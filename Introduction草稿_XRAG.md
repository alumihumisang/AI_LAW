# Introduction Draft

## English Version

Large language models (LLMs) have rapidly become an important technical foundation for legal text generation, because they can transform fragmented natural-language inputs into coherent and formal documents. In the legal domain, this capability is especially attractive for civil complaint drafting, where lawyers must organize accident facts, party relations, damages, legal provisions, and claims into a structured pleading. Traffic-accident civil complaints are a representative example of this demand. They are common in practice, highly fact-dependent, and often require the drafter to connect the accident sequence, injury descriptions, compensation items, and legal basis in a consistent manner. However, current LLM-based legal generation still faces several limitations, including hallucination, unstable legal grounding, and weak control over legal structure [6]-[9]. A general LLM may produce fluent legal language, but it may also omit important injury facts, misidentify litigant roles, cite unsuitable legal provisions, or borrow monetary amounts and factual details from unrelated examples. These problems indicate that effective traffic-accident civil complaint generation cannot rely only on the language model's parametric knowledge. It requires an external support mechanism that can provide relevant legal references while preserving the structure and factual boundaries of the input case.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) provides a natural solution to this problem by retrieving external documents before generation [1], [3]. For legal complaint drafting, RAG can help the model access prior cases, legal expressions, compensation patterns, and pleading formats, thereby reducing hallucination and improving domain adaptation. Nevertheless, conventional semantic retrieval is still insufficient for traffic-accident complaint generation. Most RAG systems retrieve documents mainly according to semantic similarity between the query and candidate texts [4], [5]. This design is useful for finding textually related cases, but it does not guarantee that the retrieved cases are legally suitable references for complaint generation. In traffic-accident disputes, two cases may share similar wording while differing significantly in injury severity, compensation structure, party relationship, or liability pattern. If such cases are directly injected into a prompt as full text, the language model may copy irrelevant facts or mix the retrieved case with the query case. Therefore, the key challenge is not merely how to retrieve similar cases, but how to retrieve and organize cases in a way that supports the legal structure of downstream complaint drafting.

Knowledge graphs offer a possible direction for improving this limitation because they can model relations among cases more explicitly than flat text retrieval [10]-[16]. Instead of treating all documents as independent chunks in a vector database, a graph-based approach can represent relations among cases, paths, and structural features, allowing retrieval to consider more than surface-level semantic overlap. Recent knowledge-graph representation methods, such as the TAARN-style approach, show the strength of combining graph structure, text descriptions, path extraction, and attention-based representation learning [2]. Such methods are valuable because they can use graph paths and textual information together to retrieve relevant cases. However, for traffic-accident civil complaint generation, a generic case graph still has a limitation: it mainly captures general graph/text relevance, such as shared legal provisions, similar compensation items, or common accident features. It does not explicitly organize neighboring cases by severity direction, nor does it directly provide lighter and heavier references for drafting facts, injuries, compensation, and legal claims. This gap motivates a more task-oriented graph structure.

To address the above problem, the proposed Severity-Aware Dual-Knowledge-Graph (SDKG) scheme supports RAG-based traffic-accident civil complaint generation through severity-ordered case relations and two severity-directional retrieval trees. The core idea is to adapt graph construction to the legal drafting task itself. Each case is treated as a complete case unit rather than being fragmented into unrelated text chunks. The complaint-style case database is based on prior data constructed from publicly available Taiwanese traffic-accident civil judgments, which were cleaned and reconstructed with the assistance of an LLM into complaint-style case materials. The proposed SDKG scheme further converts the resulting 6,057 case records into fixed boolean case profiles covering Litigants, Fact, Injury, and Compensation, and reconstructs the Fact, Injury, and Compensation dimensions into severity scores. Based on severity weights, distance weights, and thresholds, SDKG compares case pairs and keeps only sufficiently close, directionally comparable case relations. At query time, the input is mapped to an anchor case, and the light-heavy tree and heavy-light tree retrieve heavier-side and lighter-side references according to severity direction. The retrieved cases are then transformed into structured references and drafting cues before being integrated into a segmented generation pipeline covering facts, legal provisions, damages, and conclusions. In this way, the proposed SDKG scheme moves beyond flat semantic retrieval and provides controlled, severity-aware, and legally structured support for civil complaint generation.

The contributions are as follows.

1. The proposed SDKG scheme provides a severity-aware RAG framework for traffic-accident civil complaint generation to improve the controllability and legal structure of generated complaints.
2. It introduces a case-level representation and severity-ordered relation construction mechanism based on 6,057 complaint-style traffic-accident civil case records, severity weights, distance weights, and thresholds.
3. It designs an anchor-centered dual-tree retrieval process in which the light-heavy tree retrieves heavier references and the heavy-light tree retrieves lighter references, allowing the generated complaint to be supported by bidirectional severity context.
4. It builds 54 configurations from six severity-weight settings, three thresholds, and three distance weights, and prepares a 50-query evaluation setting for comparison with a TAARN-style competing method.

The paper is organized as follows. Chapter 2 reviews related works and presents the research motivation. Chapter 3 introduces the preliminaries, including the system model, problem formulation, basic idea, and comparison with competing methods. Chapter 4 describes the proposed SDKG-based RAG method in detail. Chapter 5 presents the experimental settings, evaluation results, and discussion. Finally, Chapter 6 concludes the paper and outlines future research directions.

## 中文版本

近年來，大型語言模型（Large Language Models, LLMs）迅速成為法律文本生成的重要技術基礎，原因在於其能夠將零散的自然語言輸入整理為具備一定連貫性與正式語氣的文件。在法律領域中，此一能力尤其適合用於民事起訴書撰寫，因為律師必須將事故事實、當事人關係、損害項目、法律依據與請求內容組織為具有固定結構的法律書狀。交通事故民事起訴書正是此類需求中具代表性的任務，其案件數量多、事實差異大，且撰寫時必須同時連結事故經過、傷勢描述、賠償項目與法律請求。然而，現行 LLM 用於法律書狀生成時仍存在幻覺、法律依據不穩定及法律結構控制不足等限制 [6]-[9]。一般語言模型雖可生成流暢的法律文字，但仍可能遺漏重要傷勢事實、誤判當事人角色、引用不適切法條，或從其他案例中錯誤借用金額與個案細節。這些問題顯示，交通事故民事起訴書之有效生成不能僅依賴模型內部知識，而需要外部支援機制提供相關法律參考，同時維持輸入案件本身的事實邊界與書狀結構。

檢索增強生成（Retrieval-Augmented Generation, RAG）為上述問題提供了一個自然的解決方向，因為其可在生成前先檢索外部文件，使模型取得相關案例、法律用語、賠償模式與書狀格式作為參考 [1], [3]。對於法律起訴書生成而言，RAG 有助於降低幻覺、補充領域知識，並改善大型語言模型對法律文本格式的掌握。然而，傳統語意檢索對交通事故起訴書生成仍然不足。多數 RAG 系統主要依 query 與候選文本之語意相似度進行檢索 [4], [5]，此設計雖能找到表面文字相關的案例，卻無法保證檢索結果在法律結構上適合作為起訴書生成之參考。於交通事故案件中，兩筆案件可能在文字描述上相似，卻在傷勢嚴重度、賠償結構、當事人關係或責任型態上有明顯差異。若直接將此類相似案例全文放入 prompt，模型可能複製不屬於 query 的事實，或混淆檢索案例與輸入案件。因此，關鍵問題並非僅是如何找出相似案例，而是如何以更符合法律書狀生成需求的方式，檢索並組織可用之案例參考。

知識圖譜提供了改善傳統 RAG 限制的可能方向，因為其能比平面的文本檢索更明確地表示案件之間的關係 [10]-[16]。相較於將所有文件切成獨立 chunk 後放入向量資料庫，圖譜式方法能建模案件關係、路徑與結構特徵，使檢索不只依賴表面語意重疊。近年來如 TAARN-style 方法所代表的知識圖譜表示學習，展示了結合圖結構、文字描述、路徑抽取與注意力式表示學習的能力 [2]。這類方法的優點在於能同時利用圖上路徑與案件文字，從而取得比單純語意檢索更具關聯性的案例。然而，對交通事故民事起訴書生成而言，通用案件圖仍有其限制：其主要捕捉的是案件之間的一般 graph/text relevance，例如共同法條、共同賠償項目或共同事故特徵，卻未依嚴重度方向組織鄰近案例，也未直接提供較輕與較重案例作為事實、傷勢、賠償與法律請求之撰寫參考。因此，需要的是一種更任務導向的圖結構。

為解決上述問題，所提出之 Severity-Aware Dual-Knowledge-Graph（SDKG）scheme 透過嚴重度有序之案件關係與兩棵嚴重度方向檢索樹，支援交通事故民事起訴書之 RAG 生成。其核心想法是將圖結構建構方式調整為直接服務法律書狀生成任務。每一筆案件皆被視為完整的案件單位，而非被切分為互不相關的文字片段。起訴書樣式案件資料庫以前期研究所建置之資料為基礎；該資料庫以公開交通事故民事判決書作為原始來源，並經由大型語言模型輔助清洗與重建，整理為近似起訴書格式之案件資料。所提出之 SDKG scheme 進一步將所得 6,057 筆案件資料轉換為涵蓋 Litigants、Fact、Injury 與 Compensation 四個面向之固定 boolean case profiles，並將 Fact、Injury 與 Compensation 三個面向重建為 severity scores。接著，系統依嚴重度權重、距離權重與閾值進行兩兩案件比較，只保留距離足夠接近且方向可比較的案件關係。於 query 階段，輸入案件會先被映射至 anchor case，再由 light-heavy tree 與 heavy-light tree 分別依嚴重度方向取得較重側與較輕側參考案例。檢索結果不直接以全文輸入大型語言模型，而是先轉換為結構化參考與書狀撰寫提示，再接入事實、法條、損害賠償與結論之分段式生成流程。藉由此設計，所提出之 SDKG scheme 使檢索結果不再只是平面的語意相似案例，而是能提供受控制、具嚴重度方向且符合法律結構之生成支援。

主要貢獻如下。

1. 所提出之 SDKG scheme 提供一套嚴重度感知之 RAG 架構，用於交通事故民事起訴書生成，目的在於提升生成書狀之可控性與法律結構一致性。
2. 本研究以 6,057 筆交通事故民事起訴書樣式案件資料為基礎，建立案件層級表示與嚴重度有序之案件關係建構機制，並結合嚴重度權重、距離權重與閾值形成檢索結構。
3. 本研究設計以 anchor case 為中心之 dual-tree retrieval process，其中 light-heavy tree 取得較重參考案例，heavy-light tree 取得較輕參考案例，使生成內容能獲得雙向嚴重度脈絡支援。
4. 本研究以六種嚴重度權重、三種閾值與三種距離權重建立 54 組設定，並準備 50 筆測試 query，用於後續與 TAARN-style competing method 進行比較。

其餘章節安排如下。第二章回顧相關研究並說明研究動機。第三章介紹 preliminaries，包括 system model、problem formulation、basic idea，以及與 competing methods 之比較。第四章詳細說明所提出之 SDKG-based RAG 方法。第五章呈現實驗設定、評估結果與討論。最後，第六章總結全文並提出未來研究方向。
